# Native American initiations and a traditional esoteric covenant

Research date: 4 November 2026. Language inspected: English. This batch adds one tradition record, three restricted items, two sources, three disclosures, 18 attributed claims and six artifacts. Four of the new claims expand an existing Hopi item; the already recorded unmasking claim is clarified rather than duplicated. Indigenous coverage increases to 19 tradition records, 22 items and 123 claims. These are catalogue counts, not a measure of completeness or independent corroboration.

## Particular content and restriction evidence

| Item | Inspected content | What establishes the scope, and what remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|
| **ITEM-0021**, Oraibi Powamu/Katsina initiation | Voth, printed pp. 93–94, 105, 120: instructions against disclosure even to parents, a cover explanation, different privileges following initiation, and the unmasked night dance | The chief's reported speech explicitly forbids disclosure; p. 105 distinguishes learning songs and traditions from leading dancers as naamu. Voth introduces the speech as a summary, not a verbatim transcript. The explanation of flogging as originating in punishment for a boy's disclosure is an attributed origin tradition, not an independently established event. His account of novices' prior beliefs does not establish every participant's understanding. |
| **ITEM-0061**, Zuni Ko’tikili initiation and guarded mask knowledge | Stevenson, printed pp. 102–104: godfathers, whipping, guarded mask removal, revelation of human performers, personal masks and a threat against disclosure | The source explicitly describes attendants preventing intrusion and a threat of beheading with a stone knife for revealing initiatory secrets, especially mask knowledge. The report labels the initiation witnessed in 1891. It does not establish that the threatened punishment was carried out. The author's voluntary/involuntary labels do not independently demonstrate consent. |
| **ITEM-0062**, Zuni ’Hle’wekwe Wood-fraternity initiation | Stevenson, printed pp. 451–452, 457–458: sponsored admission, preparation, prayers, medicine transmission and instruction with a wooden sword; a woman novice's different first attempt | The account identifies an initiation of a youth and maiden in 1892, with fraternity fathers and membership-specific preparation. Public festival dances and the novice sequence are distinct phases. The reported emetic rationale is not evidence of medical efficacy or safety. The medicine plant is unclassified in the cited passage; no plant identification, recipe, dose or practical swallowing instructions are supplied in the dataset. |
| **ITEM-0063**, Mithaq Wali-al-Zaman, translated Druze induction covenant | Hitti, Appendix A, printed pp. 57–58: renunciation, allegiance to al-Hakim, surrender language concerning self, possessions and children, and consequences for apostasy or disobedience | The actual translated covenant is inspected, alongside the book's description of secrecy and probation at pp. 42–43. A pledge's assertion of free will does not independently demonstrate consent. Its surrender language is not proof of a legal transfer or literal enforcement. The translation has not been collated with an Arabic manuscript or print original, and is not a complete witnessed ceremony or a modern conversion route. |

These records concern initiation content, restricted knowledge and a particular induction text. Performer identity does not independently settle the spiritual meaning of personation. Violent language in an account or pledge is not evidence of an enacted punishment. No new claim establishes present practice, a general concealment motive, or a characteristic of every member of a people or religion.

## Sources and actual inspection

- **SRC-0034**, H. R. Voth, *The Oraibi Powamu Ceremony* (1901): [publication scan](https://archive.org/details/oraibipowamucere03voth). Read printed pp. 93–94, 105 and 120; pp. 93 and 120 checked visually. Printed p. 93 is PDF p. 105, p. 105 is PDF p. 165, and p. 120 is PDF p. 216 because plates interrupt pagination. The disclosure is corrected to `partial` and reviewed for these selected passages. Stephen's separate First Mesa account, **SRC-0035 / DISC-0036**, remains unreviewed. The historical source does not substantiate the item's former undated `practiced` label, so its current status becomes `unknown`.
- **SRC-0117**, Matilda Coxe Stevenson, *The Zuni Indians: Their Mythology, Esoteric Fraternities, and Ceremonies*: [report scan](https://archive.org/details/thezueniindians00stevrich). Read selected printed pp. 102–104, 451–452 and 457–458; p. 104 checked visually. The report title page, PDF p. 7, and Archive metadata give **1904**; the [Smithsonian repository catalogue](https://repository.si.edu/items/87c1c052-ed19-4383-8eb2-9e36dc2e7bd4) instead lists **1905**. The source record uses the inspected title-page date and retains the discrepancy. The Ko’tikili account says it was witnessed in 1891; the Wood-fraternity account identifies 1892. Those dates are not publication dates. The author also says that she could not observe the plaza during the Ko’tikili initiation and supplies a separate occasion for the following plaza description; these are not merged into one observed event.
- **SRC-0118**, Philip K. Hitti, *The Origins of the Druze People and Religion, with Extracts from Their Sacred Writings* (Columbia University Press, 1928): [Appendix A](https://sacred-texts.com/book/origins-of-the-druze-people-and-religion/appendix-a-covenant-of-induction-into-the-religion-of-the-ruler-of-the-age), [chapter V](https://sacred-texts.com/book/origins-of-the-druze-people-and-religion/chapter-v-druze-theology-and-its-sources). Read the title transcription, the complete translated covenant at pp. 57–58, and the learned-rank discussion at pp. 42–43. The book's dated racial classifications, speculative generalizations and claims of an unchanging society are not adopted as facts. A hosted transcription is not a new independent manuscript witness.

All 18 new claims are `single_source`. `Reviewed` means the cited passage was checked. Publication identity and the truth of an ethnographer's interpretation remain different assessments. Neither the institution publishing a text nor a later website hosting it makes its assertions independently corroborated.
