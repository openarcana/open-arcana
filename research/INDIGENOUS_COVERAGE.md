## Remaining research priorities

1. **Review existing content before treating it as settled.** Obtain the precise editions behind unreviewed disclosures; separate observed events, attributed testimony, ritual prescriptions, staged representation and unsupported allegation. Preserve adverse evidence with its actual support level.
2. **Expand community and language coverage within the Americas.** The Southwest beyond the selected Hopi and Zuni entries, the Great Basin and California, the Subarctic, Central America, the Andes, Amazonian regions beyond the selected Pirá Paraná and upper Tapajós accounts, and other southern South American traditions remain absent or thin. Locate a named restricted item and its evidence before creating a record.
3. **Expand beyond current global entries.** New Guinea beyond the one Sambia item, much of Oceania, Siberia and most African regions have little or no coverage. Compare communities separately; a neighboring group’s initiation rules do not transfer automatically.
4. **Find actual media and original documents.** Prioritize recoverable recordings, practitioner notebooks, local publications, deposited testimony, internal texts, exposure collections and relevant dump members. Search library and shadow-library catalogues, audiovisual archives, IPFS and torrent manifests for identifiable artifacts. A metadata hit is a lead, not an inspected payload.
5. **Check first-person and community-produced material alongside outsiders’ accounts.** Include deliberate public preservation, restricted circulation and unauthorized releases. None is automatically more truthful. Trace shared informants, collectors and copied passages before labelling corroboration independent.
6. **Verify withholding and modern practice directly.** Require evidence for each suppression claim and dated evidence for each modern practice assessment. Neither an archive restriction nor a difficult search proves a general motive, a ritual’s content or a people’s disappearance.

There is no completeness percentage: no defensible denominator of all restricted knowledge has been established. Material is not excluded for discomfort or a holder’s wish for secrecy; it still needs a specific item, accurate source attribution and a support assessment that researchers and automated consumers can inspect.
