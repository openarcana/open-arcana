# Indigenous restricted-practice expansion

Research date: 4 November 2026. Text languages inspected: English and Spanish, including English translations of earlier records. Cherokee syllabary and other original-language transcriptions were not independently verified. Scope: particular restricted rites, initiations, manuscripts, names, songs and transmission rules, rather than general misconduct or a survey of entire peoples.

## Selection and result

Before this batch, the Indigenous category contained ten tradition records and eleven items. The initial batch added seven tradition records, eight items, nine sources, nine disclosures, 43 claims and nine artifact records. The final documentary review removed ITEM-0056, its two claims, its disclosure and its standalone tradition record, retaining SRC-0109 and ARTF-0017 as contextual discovery records. The final batch therefore adds six tradition records, seven items, nine sources, eight disclosures, 41 claims and nine artifacts. The category now has sixteen tradition records, eighteen items and 96 claims. These are catalogue counts, not counts of independent peoples, complete ritual systems or verified practices. The multi-community Pirá Paraná nomination remains a separate contextual source, not a counted restricted item.

The search prioritized the Americas and a specific Arctic gap, while adding content to the existing Arrernte record beyond archival-access information. Selection followed source-title searches, archive catalogues, bibliographies and references to specific ritual sections. Relevant title and topic searches included Cherokee sacred formulas, the Pawnee Hako, Osage child-naming, Selk’nam Hain and Lola Kiepja’s chants, Barasana He House/Yuruparí, Rasmussen’s Iglulik training account, and Spencer and Gillen’s churinga chapter. No claim of an exhaustive archive or shadow-library crawl is made.

Restriction and pressure are established only where a source actually documents them. An empty section, missing recording or difficult search does not establish fear, suppression or an undisclosed practice. The batch records an explicit reported attempt to stop disclosure in CLM-0179; it does not convert all gaps into suppression claims. Earlier source selections were not presumed politically motivated merely from their format or institutional origin.

## Evidence treatment

The additions include hostile ritual texts, reported secrecy threats and bodily discipline where the passages support them. They also retain participant explanations, women’s roles, acquisition negotiations, deliberate preservation and alternative paths to specialist status. No category is included or excluded merely for being uncomfortable, sacred, traditional, academic, publicly released or unauthorized.

All 41 retained new claims use `single_source`: some have multiple related publications in the surrounding item, but independent origins were not established. `reviewed` means that the cited passage was checked. Source identity, a ritual text’s content, an actual historical performance and current practice remain separate questions. Every new item’s current status is `unknown`.

The official Folkways companion and community co-produced film supply specific media-related evidence alongside textual sources. The companion’s audio and the film’s complete soundtrack remain unreviewed. They are not relabelled as leaks. No item-specific IPFS, torrent or shadow-library payload was retrieved in this batch; those routes remain eligible under the same policy.

## Access gaps and next checks

- Compare the new transcriptions against original manuscripts and stable facsimiles, and check original-language readings with competent linguistic evidence. Current summaries do not verify phonetics, translations or ritual efficacy.
- Locate the Hako cylinder catalogue records and inspect actual recordings before adding audio-content or performance claims.
- Audition the Hain album and inspect its track timing, credits and recording context. Do not use a shared investigator as a second independent origin.
- Inspect Hugh-Jones’s original Barasana fieldwork volume and the complete Yuruparí film/nomination material. The UNESCO heritage page returned a CAPTCHA; it was not solved or bypassed. The public YouTube route remained available. The film does not independently verify all details in the older Barasana account.
- Revisit the existing Hopi, Midewiwin, Hamatsa, Ngarrindjeri, Pitjantjatjara and Strehlow records at their actual cited editions. Their old disclosure-review labels were not silently upgraded by this batch.
- Establish dated evidence for present-day practice separately; avoid treating old salvage narratives as proof that a people has disappeared.
