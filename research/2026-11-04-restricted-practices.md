# Restricted practices: source inspection and selection

Research date: 4 November 2026. Language inspected: English, including English transcriptions and translations. This is a bounded research batch, not a systematic review or evidence that other traditions lack comparable material.

## Question and scope

What specific restricted initiations, ritual instructions and teachings are missing or thinly described in the seed, including coercive, sexual or otherwise uncomfortable material?

The object of a record must be a restricted rite, initiation, practice, teaching, text or transmission. General wrongdoing does not qualify merely because it was concealed. After that scope was clarified, unpublished draft additions about Catholic investigation confidentiality and FLDS case records were removed from this batch. The DOS entry was narrowed to admission collateral, concealed leadership and the branding ceremony; individual assaults and general conviction claims were removed. These were relevance decisions, not findings that the discarded sources were false.

The batch adds four traditions, four initiation items, seven sources, nine disclosure records and 30 claims. It also expands the existing O.T.O. item. New practice statuses remain unknown. All new claims have source locators and review notes; none is labelled independently corroborated.

## Collections and searches

- Official OHCHR, UNMIL and UN Liberia publications: reports and testimony concerning Sande/Poro initiation, restrictions on observation and disclosure, and coercive incorporation.
- A Second Circuit opinion republished by Justia: DOS admission conditions, branding design and prescribed ceremony speech.
- Hosted Crowley transcriptions and a university-hosted scholarly article: O.T.O. IX° instructions, sexual degree teachings, sacramental fluids and coded liturgical language.
- The Wikipedia Library’s discovery service: query `Aghor Medicine`. Search results included the book and several reviews. The authorized JSTOR page visibly confirmed access provided by Wikipedia. William S. Sax’s review was read on pp. 859–860; the book itself was not read, and no book-content claim was added from its publisher description or review alone.
- The review page linked Henry Balfour’s historical article. Further browser navigation failed. A public search for `Henry Balfour "Life History of an Aghori Fakir" 1897 full text` located a scan; the article’s relevant pages were then inspected directly. The Pitt Rivers Museum’s bibliography was used to check publication details.

Scite returned an access/subscription limitation rather than usable literature. The Wikipedia Library was an additional authorized route, not evidence that every listed work was accessible or read. This note records consequential access limits rather than an exhaustive browser history. No account credentials or personal access URLs are included.

## Next evidence gaps

- Obtain fuller descriptions of Sande/Poro internal teaching and ritual actions, with precise local and temporal scope, rather than expanding the catalogue through incident counts alone.
- Collate the Crowley transcriptions with the identified Yorke manuscripts and inspect the cited E.G.C. manual directly.
- Inspect Barrett’s book and the initiation material Balfour cites in H. W. Barrow’s 1893 paper. Distinguish individual practice, other people’s allegations and later reforms; neither old generalizations nor modern publicity settles them.
- Seek practitioner explanations, counterevidence, independent origins and dated evidence of subsequent change. These sources do not establish present-day practice or representative coverage.
