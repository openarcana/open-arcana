# Further restricted rites: field accounts and exposed files

Research date: 4 November 2026. Language inspected: English. This batch adds four specifically identified items, three tradition records, seven sources, seven disclosures, 26 attributed claims and eight artifacts. None of the new claims asserts current practice. It is a bounded expansion, not an inventory of all hidden knowledge.

## Findings and scope evidence

| Item | Why it qualifies | What the inspected source establishes, and its limits |
|---|---|---|
| **ITEM-0057**, Long Valley Patwin thunder/kuksu initiation and later hesi incorporation | Loeb explicitly describes initiation and restricted attendance, and distinguishes concealed representation from scenes witnessed outside | [Loeb’s scan](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/82905/files/ucp033-003.pdf), pp. 223–225. Feigned death and reported blood-drawing injury are distinct. The introduction identifies 1931 interviews with Wilson Lewis and his wife; the account is retrospective, not Loeb witnessing the historical rite. Earlier and later forms, and other Patwin communities, remain distinct. |
| **ITEM-0058**, Sambia age-graded male initiation | Herdt explicitly states that particular procedures were withheld from women, children and junior initiates until successive revelations | [1982 fieldwork report](https://www.faculty.fairfield.edu/dcrawford/herdt.pdf), especially p. 200. The dataset records bodily coercion and sexual rites involving minors without graphic sexual details. The [2011 retrospective](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p146101/html/Text/ch12.html?page=17&referer=189) distinguishes secrecy from ordinary privacy and limits its ethnographic focus to 1993, noting subsequent radical change. The two works share an investigator and upstream research. |
| **ITEM-0059**, LDS second anointing | A participant reports secrecy instructions and specific ceremony content; a separate official manual explicitly restricts classroom discussion | [Phillips’s archived account](https://bhroberts.org/records/fWrZbd-17raDb/tom_phillips_provides_a_contemporary_account_of_the_second_anointing) describes a claimed 2002 event in a 2008 recollection. The [church manual](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/doctrines-of-the-gospel/chapter-19?lang=eng) establishes the discussion restriction, but does not authenticate Phillips’s event or every procedural detail. His wording was refreshed from published accounts. |
| **ITEM-0060**, exposed Gardnerian first-degree text and preservation warning | The actual script contains an oath restricting revelation and a warning about custody of writings | [Mirrored Global Grey PDF](https://priory-of-sion.com/biblios/images/Gardnerian-Book-Of-Shadows.pdf), printed pp. 22, 43–45. The text prescribes ordeals and concealment; it does not prove performance, consent or a uniform practice across covens. The [editorial index](https://sacred.plzhalp.us/pag/gbos/index.htm) describes a 1990s Internet compilation and estimated section dates. Authenticity and manuscript fidelity remain uncertain. |

These are disclosures of particular content or rules, not general scandals. Their historical occurrence, the fidelity of an exposed copy, and any claimed ritual efficacy are separate questions. All new claims use `single_source`; additional publications are not counted as independent merely because they have different hosts or dates. The official manual's narrow instruction is its own claim, rather than being used to corroborate the whole participant account.

## Routes searched and files inspected

Discovery used searches for the Eastern Kuksu cult, Sambia initiation and nosebleeding, the second anointing and Tom Phillips, exposed Gardnerian initiations, Ankave ritual secrecy, and Guardians of the Flutes in shadow-library indexes. Full-text candidates were selected for evidence of an actual restriction or initiation, not merely a promising title.

The unconventional routes used here include an occult site's mirrored PDF, an exposed-text mirror, a faculty-hosted scanned article and a former participant's text preserved by a separate archive. Institutional documents were checked alongside them. Hosting category is not a truth score, and neither the occult mirror nor an Internet compilation was relabelled as an authenticated insider leak.

## Unsuccessful or limited routes

- The alternative-host PDF of The Social Life of Trees timed out. An indexed Ankave passage was a lead, not a retrieved file or a verified new item in this batch.
- Searches for Guardians of the Flutes in shadow-library indexes yielded no inspected new book payload. The faculty scan is a different 1982 article, not that book or the similarly titled film. No IPFS or torrent payload was retrieved or verified here.
- The scanned article describes distress and contains a footnoted criticism of earlier researchers playing down fear. That is retained as an attributed criticism; it does not prove a named suppressed publication or a general politically motivated omission.
- The exposed text's estimated composition dates are not its publication dates. The script's persecution narrative does not establish the historical continuity it implies.
- No recording was auditioned or added as inspected audiovisual evidence in this batch. The files actually checked are identified above.

Remaining checks include original manuscript collation for the exposed text, independent evidence of the participant's ceremony, comparisons with local and later accounts for both Indigenous cases, and dated modern-practice evidence. Current status stays `unknown` for all four items.
